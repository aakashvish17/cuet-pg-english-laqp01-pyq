1. T.S. Eliot — "Dissociation of Sensibility"

Reappeared 5 Times 🔥 High Priority
Chronological Trail: CUCET 2017 → DUET 2019 → CUET-PG 2022 (Slot 1) → CUET-PG 2023 (Shift 2) → CUET-PG 2026 (Shift 3)
Exam Variations:
• CUET-PG 2026: "The phrase 'Dissociation of Sensibility' was coined by T.S. Eliot in his critical essay titled: (1) Tradition and the Individual Talent (2) Hamlet and His Problems (3) The Metaphysical Poets (4) The Function of Criticism"
• CUET-PG 2022 / CUCET 2017: "In which century according to T.S. Eliot did a dissociation of sensibility set in? (1) 16th century (2) 17th century (3) 18th century (4) 19th century"
Verified Answer & Theoretical Context:
Coined in "The Metaphysical Poets" (1921). Eliot argued that in writers like John Donne and Andrew Marvell, thought and emotion were unified ("they felt their thought as immediately as the odour of a rose"). During the 17th century, a split occurred where language grew refined while feeling crude. Eliot held John Milton and John Dryden responsible for compounding this separation.
Topic: Literary Criticism & Modernist Poetics

2. John Keats — "Negative Capability"

Clockwork 2-Year Pulse 5 Cycles
Chronological Trail: DUET 2018 → CUCET 2020 → CUET-PG 2022 → CUET-PG 2024 → CUET-PG 2026 (Every Alternate Year!)
Exam Variations:
• CUET-PG 2026 / 2024: "Who coined the phrase 'Negative Capability' to describe an author capable of being in uncertainties, mysteries, doubts without any irritable reaching after fact and reason?"
• DUET 2018 / CUET 2022: "In his December 1817 letter to his brothers, whom did John Keats cite as the supreme exemplar of Negative Capability? (1) Milton (2) Wordsworth (3) Shakespeare (4) Coleridge"
Verified Answer & Key Formulation:
Formulated in an 1817 letter to his brothers George and Thomas Keats. Defined as the capacity to negate one's own ego and exist within doubts and ambiguities without intellectual insistence. Supreme model: William Shakespeare. Keats contrasted this with Coleridge, whom he criticized for incapable surrender to mystery due to excessive intellectualization.
Topic: Romantic Aesthetics & Poetic Theory

3. S.T. Coleridge — "Fancy vs. Imagination"

Reappeared 5 Times Biographia Literaria Ch. 13
Chronological Trail: CUCET 2018 → DUET 2021 → CUET-PG 2022 → CUET-PG 2023 → CUET-PG 2026
Exam Question:
"In Biographia Literaria (1817), Coleridge distinguishes between Primary and Secondary Imagination. Secondary Imagination differs from Primary because it:
(1) Dissolves, diffuses, dissipates in order to recreate
(2) Is the living power and prime agent of all human perception
(3) Operates only through memory and mechanical association
(4) Belongs exclusively to children"
Verified Answer & Theoretical Distinctions:
Correct Option: (1) Dissolves, diffuses, dissipates in order to recreate.
• Primary Imagination: Universal perceptual agency shared by all human beings.
• Secondary Imagination: Co-existing with conscious will, it is the creative poetic faculty that unifies and idealizes.
• Fancy: Merely mechanical aggregator of existing fixities and memories.
Topic: Romantic Criticism

4. Aristotle — "Poetics" Core Tragic Terminology

Nearly Every Cycle Top Match Table Item
Chronological Trail: BHU PET 2016 → CUCET 2017 → DUET 2019 → CUET-PG 2022 → CUET-PG 2023 → CUET-PG 2025 → CUET-PG 2026
Standard Matching Matrix:
List I: (A) Hamartia (B) Catharsis (C) Anagnorisis (D) Peripeteia
List II: (1) Recognition from ignorance (2) Tragic flaw or error of judgment (3) Sudden reversal of fortune (4) Purgation of pity and fear
Verified Answer & Definitions:
• Hamartia → Tragic error in judgment / fatal miscalculation.
• Catharsis → Purgation / clarification of pity (eleos) and fear (phobos).
• Anagnorisis → Discovery / recognition from ignorance to knowledge.
• Peripeteia → Reversal of situation / ironic turning point.
Topic: Classical Literary Theory

5. Mulk Raj Anand — "Untouchable" (1935)

2-Year Alternate Cycle 5 Appearances
Chronological Trail: CUCET 2018 → DUET 2020 → CUET-PG 2022 → CUET-PG 2024 → CUET-PG 2026
Tested Exam Dimensions:
1. Protagonist identity and location.
2. Novel's temporal structure.
3. Famous British author who wrote the preface.
4. Three distinct remedies offered at the climax.
Verified Facts:
• Protagonist: Bakha (18-year-old sweeper boy in Bulashah cantonment).
• Temporal Span: Compressed entirely into a single day (influenced by James Joyce's Ulysses).
• Preface: Written by E.M. Forster.
• Three Ending Solutions: Introduction of the modern flush system / machine, conversion to Christianity, and Mahatma Gandhi's moral upliftment.

8. New Criticism — Wimsatt & Beardsley Fallacies

Strict 2-Year Pulse 4 Appearances
Chronological Trail: CUCET 2019 → DUET 2021 → CUET-PG 2023 → CUET-PG 2025
Key Distinctions Tested:
• Meaning of "Intentional Fallacy" (1946).
• Meaning of "Affective Fallacy" (1949).
• Title of the anthology containing both foundational essays.
Verified Answer:
• Intentional Fallacy (1946): The critical mistake of seeking to evaluate a poem based on the author's psychological intention.
• Affective Fallacy (1949): The mistake of judging a poem by its emotional and psychological effect upon the reader.
• Core Volume: The Verbal Icon: Studies in the Meaning of Poetry (1954).
Topic: New Criticism & Formalist Theory

10. Rhetoric: Metonymy vs. Synecdoche vs. Paradox vs. Oxymoron

Every Single Year (2014–2026) 100% Recurrence Guarantee
Chronological Trail: Tested across all shifts without exception in direct definition or Match List-I & List-II format.
Core Distinctions:
• Oxymoron: Side-by-side contradictory terms (e.g., "bittersweet", Milton's "darkness visible").
• Paradox: A self-contradictory proposition revealing a deeper resolution (Wordsworth's "The Child is father of the Man").
• Metonymy: Substitution by contiguous association ("The Crown" for Monarchy; "The Pen" for writing).
• Synecdoche: Part representing the whole ("All hands on deck"; "Wheels" for vehicle).
Exam Scoring Tip:
If List-I features figures of speech, finding either Metonymy or Oxymoron immediately eliminates 3 of the 4 options, securing instant +4 marks without solving all four.
Topic: Literary Terms & Rhetorical Devices

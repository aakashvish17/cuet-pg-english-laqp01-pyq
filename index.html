1. T.S. Eliot — "Dissociation of Sensibility" Essay & Century ▲
🎯 Answer :- Option (3) "The Metaphysical Poets" (1921) | Century: 17th Century

Appeared in: CUCET 2017, DUET 2019, CUET-PG 2022 (Slot 1), CUET-PG 2023 (Shift 2), CUET-PG 2026 (Shift 3) • Recurred 5 times

Historical Context & Core Analysis:
• Essay: Coined in "The Metaphysical Poets" (1921) reviewing Grierson's anthology.
• Century: Occurred during the 17th century after Donne and Herbert.
• Blamed Authors: Eliot directly blamed John Milton and John Dryden for separating thought from feeling.
2. John Keats — "Negative Capability" Origin & Supreme Exemplar ▲
🎯 Answer :- John Keats (December 1817 Letter) | Supreme Model: Option (3) William Shakespeare

Appeared in: BHU 2014, DUET 2018, CUCET 2020, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2024, CUET-PG 2026 • Exact 2-Year Alternate Cycle!

Key Formulation & Keats's Words:
• Formulated in a December 1817 letter to brothers George and Thomas Keats.
• Definition: "When a man is capable of being in uncertainties, mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching after fact and reason."
• Supreme Model: William Shakespeare (contrasted with Coleridge).
3. S.T. Coleridge — Primary vs. Secondary Imagination vs. Fancy ▲
🎯 Answer :- Option (1) Dissolves, diffuses, dissipates in order to recreate

Appeared in: CUCET 2018, DUET 2021, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2023, CUET-PG 2026 • High Frequency

Core Text & Distinctions (Biographia Literaria, 1817, Ch. 13):
• Primary Imagination: Living power and prime agent of all human perception; common to all humans.
• Secondary Imagination: An echo of primary, co-existing with conscious will: "It dissolves, diffuses, dissipates, in order to recreate." Vital and synthetic.
• Fancy: Merely a mechanical memory faculty dealing only with fixities and definities.
4. Aristotle's Poetics — Four Formative Tragic Terms ▲
🎯 Answer :- Hamartia → (2) Tragic flaw/error | Catharsis → (4) Purgation of pity/fear | Anagnorisis → (1) Recognition | Peripeteia → (3) Reversal of fortune

Appeared in: BHU PET 2016, CUCET 2017, DUET 2019, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2023, CUET-PG 2025, CUET-PG 2026

Matching Matrix & Standard Definitions:
• Hamartia: Tragic flaw or error of judgment by the hero.
• Catharsis: Purgation / purification of pity (eleos) and fear (phobos).
• Anagnorisis: The shift from ignorance to knowledge / moment of recognition.
• Peripeteia: Sudden reversal of fortune or circumstance.
5. Mulk Raj Anand — "Untouchable" (1935) Tested Dimensions ▲
🎯 Answer :- Untouchable (1935) | Protagonist: Bakha | Preface: E.M. Forster | Ending Solution: Flush System / Machine

Appeared in: CUCET 2018, DUET 2020, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2024, CUET-PG 2026 • Consistent 2-Year Pulse

Four Crucial Exam Facts:
1. Protagonist: Bakha (an 18-year-old sweeper boy in Bulashah).
2. Time Frame: Unfolds over a single day (influence of James Joyce's Ulysses).
3. Preface: Written by Bloomsbury author E.M. Forster.
4. Three Proposed Solutions: Flush toilet / machine (advocated by poet Iqbal Nath Sarshar), conversion to Christianity, and Mahatma Gandhi's moral plea.
6. Raja Rao — "Kanthapura" (1938) Narrative Technique & Foreword ▲
🎯 Answer :- Option (3) Achakka (old grandmother) | Narrative Technique: Sthala-Purana & Harikatha

Appeared in: CUCET 2017, DUET 2019, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2025, CUET-PG 2026

Core Concepts:
• Narrator: Narrated by elderly village grandmother Achakka.
• Genre & Mode: Uses traditional Indian oral storytelling: Sthala-Purana (legendary history of a place) and Harikatha.
• Movement Leader: Moorthy, who brings Gandhian non-violent resistance to the village.
• Author's Foreword: Explores the challenge of writing Indian sensibility and tempo in English: "We cannot write like the English. We should not. We cannot write only as Indians."

8. New Criticism — W.K. Wimsatt & Monroe Beardsley Fallacies ▲
🎯 Answer :- Option (2) Intentional Fallacy (1946) | Affective Fallacy (1949) | Book: The Verbal Icon (1954)

Appeared in: CUCET 2019, DUET 2021, CUET-PG 2023, CUET-PG 2025 • Strict 2-Year Interval

Key Essays & The Verbal Icon (1954):
• Intentional Fallacy (1946): The error of judging the meaning or success of a work of literature by evaluating the author's intended purpose.
• Affective Fallacy (1949): The confusion between the poem itself and its emotional results on the reader (relativism).
• Tenet: The text is an autonomous verbal icon; poem belongs neither to author nor reader once published.
9. Postcolonial Theory — Edward Said's "Orientalism" (1978) ▲
🎯 Answer :- Option (2) Orientalism (1978) | Influencing Thinker: Michel Foucault (Discourse & Power/Knowledge)

Appeared in: DUET 2018, CUCET 2021, CUET-PG 2023, CUET-PG 2024, CUET-PG 2026

Key Conceptual Dimensions:
• Theoretical Influence: Applied Michel Foucault's concept of Discourse and Power/Knowledge to colonial representation.
• Core Thesis: "The Orient" is not an inert natural reality, but a European cultural, political, and textual invention created to define the West by contrast.
• Key Epigraphs: Uses Karl Marx ("They cannot represent themselves; they must be represented") and Benjamin Disraeli.
10. Figures of Speech: Metonymy vs. Synecdoche vs. Paradox vs. Oxymoron ▲
🎯 Answer :- Synecdoche = Part for whole | Metonymy = Contiguous substitute | Oxymoron = Adjacent contradiction | Paradox = Self-contradictory truth

Appeared in: Every single year (2014–2026) in definitions or matching tables

Essential Definitions to Memorize:
• Oxymoron: Two contradictory terms juxtaposed side-by-side (e.g., "bittersweet", "deafening silence", Milton's "darkness visible").
• Paradox: A statement that appears self-contradictory on the surface, but reveals an underlying truth (e.g., Wordsworth's "The Child is father of the Man"; Donne's "Death, thou shalt die").
• Metonymy: Replaces the name of an entity with something closely associated with it (e.g., "The Pen is mightier than the Sword"; "The Crown" for Monarchy).
• Synecdoche: A part of something is made to represent the whole, or the whole represents a part (e.g., "All hands on deck" [hands = sailors]; "new wheels" [wheels = car]).
11. Kamala Das — Confessional Poetry & "An Introduction" Opening Lines ▲
🎯 Answer :- An Introduction by Kamala Das (The Old Playhouse and Other Poems)

Appeared in: CUCET 2018, DUET 2020, CUET-PG 2023, CUET-PG 2026 • Tested on lines: "I don't know politics but I know the names of those in power..."

Key Conceptual Dimensions:
• Collection: Included in The Old Playhouse and Other Poems (1973) and Summer in Calcutta (1965).
• Linguistic Defiance: "The language I speak, / Becomes mine, its distortions, its queernesses / All mine, mine alone."
• Pen Name: Wrote in Malayalam under the pen name Madhavikutty.
12. William Wordsworth — Definition of Poetry & "Emotion Recollected in Tranquillity" ▲
🎯 Answer :- William Wordsworth (Preface to Lyrical Ballads, 1800 Edition)

Appeared in: JNUEE 2015, CUCET 2017, DUET 2019, CUET-PG 2022, CUET-PG 2025 • Foundational Romantic Doctrine

Verbatim Formula to Memorize:
• "Poetry is the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings: it takes its origin from emotion recollected in tranquillity."
• Language Doctrine: Advocated "a selection of language really used by men" in humble and rustic life, rejecting 18th-century poetic diction.
